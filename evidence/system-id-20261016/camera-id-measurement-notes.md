Required measurements before metric dynamic identification:

1. Independent physical scale/geometry: at least two measured cart positions separated by a known physical distance, using the pivot projection along the rail; more positions can reveal perspective/nonlinearity. A ruler or known marker-center spacing in the same plane is useful. Do not calibrate the camera solely from commanded step distance when the purpose is to independently test motor tracking, and do not equate physical pivot-to-bob marker spacing with dynamic effective pendulum length.
2. Timing: preserve Jetson monotonic camera t (assigned after cap.read) and host serial receive timestamps together with board ticks. When processes share the Jetson kernel, host monotonic times share a clock; board time still needs an offset/drift fit and transport-age accounting. Separately estimate camera exposure/delivery delay and its variation. A dynamics fit can estimate a time shift, but must report the resulting uncertainty rather than assuming zero latency.
3. Stationary and moving quality: estimate pivot/angle scatter from stationary data, then test length consistency, rail cross-track, finite values, frame freshness, sequence gaps and pose continuity during the intended motion. Use the raw endpoint trace and occasional frames to classify dropouts. The detector confidence is a geometry score, not a calibrated error probability. Set gates from measured distributions; do not blindly relax the existing length/support gates.
4. Input/output separation: retain requested acceleration, MCU governed acceleration/velocity/count position, raw encoder angle and filtered encoder angle separately from optical pivot displacement and pole angle. Fit physical motion from camera position/angle with a stated smoothing or dynamics model; raw frame differencing can amplify pixel noise. Keep invalid intervals out of fits instead of treating held/interpolated samples as fresh measurements.
