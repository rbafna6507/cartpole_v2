## What this distinguishes

The reader was **not completely starved** for the whole failed step: it received and processed ticks 219, 223, 227 and 231, sent a PING and sent emergency STOP. Its receipt spacing was nevertheless disturbed: 8.462630 ms from ticks 215→219, then 5.409238, 3.216755 and 0.563913 ms for the next three nominal 4 ms frames. Receipt-to-state-record delays of 4.997 and 2.421 ms also show intermittent delay within the reader's state processing. These timestamps occur after line decoding and before `_accept`; they are not USB packet arrival timestamps.

The SDK records a command **after** `transport.write()` returns and after releasing `_write_lock`. Here, generic `ERR mode` was recorded **5.501175 ms before** the A684 command record. A684 is the unique pending action consistent with that rejection, although the untagged ERR cannot prove the association by itself. Consequently, the late command record must not be interpreted as the actual send time. An earlier A679 is explicitly acknowledged in telemetry before its command record as well. The write lock was available to the reader for PING and STOP during the broad stalled interval; that interval therefore cannot all be one uninterrupted main-thread ownership of the write lock.

There are **no write-lock acquisition timestamps, transport syscall entry/exit timestamps, per-stage thread CPU measurements or scheduler traces** in this recording. The evidence supports intermittent delay affecting both main and reader paths, but does not resolve scheduler/GIL waiting, lock acquisition, pyserial's write/select behavior, mirror/state processing, or USB/driver delay into a unique cause. The separate source investigation of pyserial is a hypothesis to test, not a finding proven by this log.
