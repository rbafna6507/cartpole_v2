### 1. Cart motion is not tracking the software's position estimate

The user reports barely moving, with the motor buzzing/vibrating. From 12.2–16 s, the encoder angle spans only about 0.18°, despite large commanded cart movements and speed up to 1.3395 m/s. This is strong evidence of an actuator-following problem. Stalling/loss of synchronism is the leading explanation for the reported buzzing; insufficient current/voltage, incorrect coil wiring, mechanical binding, driver logic/pulse problems, or aggressive motion demands can cause similar symptoms. The CSV cannot distinguish those causes.

`ax_pos` counts scheduled step pulses in the ISR. It does not measure motor rotation or cart displacement. The reported velocity and acceleration are commands. No cart encoder or stall signal exists in these logs. The software can therefore traverse its entire virtual rail while the physical cart hardly moves; its braking then responds to that incorrect position estimate. No logged fault means only that the existing software checks did not trip.

### 2. The changed limits did not make ordinary energy pumping stronger

With `ke=1`, the normal energy-shaping acceleration near hanging is about ±2 m/s² (`a = ke * (normalized_energy - 1) * sign`). `amax_s=10` is only a ceiling and a stalled-pendulum kick amplitude. Normal pumping used at most 2.1 m/s² in about 93% of the recorded swing-up samples. The separate braking override can demand 18 m/s².

Acceleration command chatter also appears: 18–21 s contains about 29 sampled acceleration sign changes per second and 17 sampled velocity sign changes per second. This is a further contributor to buzzing and inefficient excitation. Once actual motor tracking is established, the noisy sign-based energy pump, estimator, kick logic, and rail override need joint validation; increasing the ceiling alone does not solve them.

The prior native tests checked motion-command limits and braking under ideal motor tracking. They did not validate physical torque, missed steps, or closed-loop swing-up/balance performance. The high acceleration defaults were not established by a motor/load test.

### 3. The pendulum model is stale

The user confirms the physical length changed to 175 mm after the free-decay calibration. Firmware still uses the old effective length of 0.127 m and derives its balance gains from it. `cartpole_controller_config.json` now marks that measurement invalid for the current geometry. Its historical value is retained rather than replaced with an invented measurement.

Repeat free-decay characterization with the current rod and weights. Physical length and dynamic effective length are different quantities, so `leff` should not simply become 0.175. Updating a JSON file does not update the flashed sketch.

### 4. The 60T pulley is already configured

For a 2 mm-pitch belt, a 60T pulley moves 120 mm per revolution. With a 200-step/revolution motor at 1/16 microstepping, that is 26.6667 pulses/mm. A 1.5 m/s command requires 750 rpm and 40,000 pulses/s. The current programmed pulse ceiling is 62,500/s, so there is nominal pulse-rate headroom. This arithmetic does not prove the driver outputs correct pulses or the loaded motor can produce enough torque.

The larger pulley gives three times the travel and one-third the belt force for the same shaft torque compared with a 20T pulley. Motor torque at speed and cart mass matter. NEMA 17 frame size does not supply those values.

The code's 24 V / Big Easy Driver / 1:16 assumptions are not measurements. Manufacturer references: [SparkFun Big Easy Driver](https://www.sparkfun.com/big-easy-driver.html), [board schematic](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/datasheets/Robotics/BigEasyDriver_v16a.pdf).
2. Check low-speed manual travel with a ruler, after physically recentering and resetting the position reference. Verify the actual motor shaft, pulley, belt and cart follow together. Start at 20 mm/s and 0.2 m/s², with 5 m/s³ jerk, for this diagnostic only. If that still buzzes, resolve drive wiring/current/logic or mechanical binding before raising control gains. If it works, progressively measure the load's acceleration/speed limits and bidirectional repeatability.
3. Record moving cart mass and check belt tension/rail drag. An oscilloscope or logic analyzer can establish STEP/DIR timing if the electrical drive remains suspect; the software log cannot.
4. Rerun free-swing calibration with the cart fixed and the current 175 mm configuration. Verify encoder sign against physical cart direction. Only then retune and validate the motor and pendulum together.
